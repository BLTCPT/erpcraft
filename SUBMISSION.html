Project name

ERPCraft

Tagline

A free, open-source, clean-room ERP in Rust — AI-native from day one, with a double-entry ledger the database itself refuses to let you break.

What it does

ERPCraft is an enterprise resource planning system for the world its builder actually lives in: small manufacturers who run their businesses on spreadsheets because the incumbent ERPs are closed, expensive, and held together by consultants — and who are now being asked to trust AI agents with real books.

So ERPCraft is built around one idea: an AI agent should drive the exact same commands a human does, propose before it posts, and work on top of a ledger whose correctness does not depend on any caller behaving — every invariant is enforced by the database itself, and the enforcement has been probe-tested from outside, including with raw SQL.

The vision is the full ERP: general ledger, order-to-cash, purchasing, inventory, manufacturing planning, and reporting, owned by the people who run it. This repository is the working core of that vision, built and verified in the open:

  • A double-entry ledger enforced by the database itself. Every posting must balance (debits = credits) — the rule lives in SQLite triggers, not application code, so entries can only be created as drafts and post through the one balance-checked transition; direct posted inserts are refused, amounts are integer cents at rest (a fractional “cent” cannot be stored, let alone posted), and on application connections the append-only guards also fire on the implicit deletes of INSERT OR REPLACE, so history cannot be rewritten through that path either. The journal is append-only; corrections are reversing entries that reference the original. Money is integer cents, never floating point. An external review probed exactly these boundaries with executable tests; every hole it found is fixed and now covered by regression tests in the suite.
  • Order-to-cash with a GL posting engine. Quotes → sales orders → invoices → payments → returns, where the transitions themselves generate the revenue, COGS, payment, and reversal postings. Correctness is pinned by a hand-computed “oracle day”: 14 transactions whose ending trial balance ($129,975.00 per side) is the acceptance suite.
  • A variant configurator modeled on SAP LO-VC object dependencies — precondition, selection condition, and constraint — as a typed AST, evaluated deterministically in the core. Selected components roll into line price and standard cost. AI works only at the edges: it authors dependency rules behind a propose → confirm gate and explains refusals in plain language. Ask for a galvanized finish on an aluminum bracket and the system refuses, naming the rule: “Galvanized finish is available only on steel brackets.”
  • Procure-to-pay with a three-way match. The buying mirror of order-to-cash: suppliers → requisitions (approved before they convert) → purchase orders → goods receipts (Dr inventory / Cr GR-IR clearing) → supplier invoices (GR-IR clears at the PO price; the difference posts to purchase price variance) → payments. The three-way match is computed live from the three documents on every read: invoice more than was received, or at any price but the PO price (zero price tolerance, deliberately — any difference is a variance), and the invoice lands on hold — recorded, visible, and unpayable until the variance is resolved or a named person overrides it with a recorded reason (the variance facts stay on the invoice either way).
  • Controls foundations. Fiscal periods gate the single posting path — close a period and every door refuses postings dated into it, rolling the whole document transition back. A users/roles registry backs a segregation-of-duties rule: payments above a configured threshold require a named actor and a different approver with an approver/admin role, recorded on the payment and its journal entry. The rule gates the capability, not just the document methods: the generic cash-out journal builders enforce it too. The supplier’s own invoice number is also a duplicate key in procure-to-pay: the same number against the same PO can never be recorded — and so never paid — twice. (See ENTERPRISE_GAPS.md for the honest map of what enterprise scale still needs beyond this, and in what order.)
  • One dispatch table, four doors. 85 commands served identically to an operator CLI, an MCP server on stdio, an HTTP API, and the natural-language agent — JSON out, plain-language refusals, exit codes 0/1/2. Anything a human can do, an agent can drive; there is no second, weaker API. (The HTTP door binds 127.0.0.1 and is a trusted local operator door, like the CLI; the propose → confirm gate is the agent surface’s contract, and the segregation-of-duties rule applies at every door.)
  • A natural-language agent behind a propose → confirm gate. Plain English (“Invoice Acme for the 40 units we shipped Tuesday”) becomes proposed dispatch calls. The confirmation is rendered from actual ledger data — never from model prose — and nothing posts until a human confirms. The agent also answers questions, can propose releasing a plan, receives goods against purchase orders, and pays supplier invoices — including the held ones, where a payment can only be proposed when the prompt itself carries the match-override reason and the approver’s name — enforced in code, not just documented: override attribution the model supplies that does not appear in the human’s own words is discarded. Confirmations are one-shot (a confirmed action cannot be replayed) and revalidated at confirm time, and every confirmation is rendered from the actual document it will post — quantity, price, configuration.
  • AI-native MRP on the Warp Speed pattern. Materials planning that is dynamic (every run recomputes from current orders, stock, BOMs, and configurations — plans are never stored, so a changed order re-plans instantly), explainable (every planned order carries its netting math and demand reasons; a shortage is simply a planned order whose start date has already passed), and agent-native (“Can we build 200 BRK-100 by Nov 15?” is answered from a live run; releasing a planned order goes through the same confirm gate and creates the document — planning never posts to the GL).
  • A full audit trail. Every journal entry explains itself: who posted it, which document it belongs to, why it posted, the complete provenance chain (quote → order → invoice → payments → returns) with reversal links in both directions — and, for agent postings, the verbatim human prompt that proposed it.

The demo company is Cascade Precision Parts, a fictional small Oregon manufacturer making metal brackets.

How it was built

Pure Rust, three crates in one workspace:

  • erpcraft-core — the deterministic core: ledger schema and invariant triggers, the posting engine, order-to-cash, procure-to-pay, the controls foundations (fiscal periods, approval rule), the variant configurator, the audit trail, and MRP. The evaluator and the planner are pure functions of current state.
  • erpcraft-server — an Axum HTTP API over the core.
  • erpcraft-cli — the dispatch table (CLI + MCP doors) and the natural-language agent adapter over it.

Design rules decided once and held throughout: integer cents everywhere; double-entry enforced in the DB layer; the journal is append-only and corrections are reversing entries; agents propose, humans confirm, then it posts; deterministic core with AI at the edges (models parse, author behind the gate, and explain — they never evaluate the configurator or compute a plan); every command has a test, and the hand-computed oracle-day trial balance is the acceptance suite. 165 automated tests are green.

The AI author behind this project works professionally in SAP variant configuration (LO-VC) — the configurator’s dependency semantics come from that world experience, re-implemented clean-room as typed data rather than stringly-typed syntax.

Partner technology

  • Baseten — Muse Spark Open (meta-models/muse-spark-open-1026), used live. The agent’s live parse route runs on Baseten (hackathon grant). The shipped demo fixture was recorded from the actual model (schema-valid output). On Oct 31 the evaluation went the whole distance: all 23 agent acceptance scenarios — the 12 contract prompts, both MRP intents, the configurator what-if refusal, and the procure-to-pay intents — were sent to the live model and replayed through the real binary; every one landed on the exact contract outcome (postings, refusals, balances). The full accounting, including the model’s stochastic reasoning-loop failures and the two prompt fixes they produced, is in LIVE_EVAL.md. Total live usage: ~150,600 tokens against the grant.
  • Meta Model API — wired as a route. The agent’s client supports the Meta Model API (--client meta) alongside Baseten, the recorded fixture, and a deterministic offline stub, which remains the default so the demo and the test suite run with no network at all. The route’s key verifies live; the $150 credit was never confirmed as landed in the account, so no spend was run against it.

What to try (5-minute demo)

From the repo root, with a fresh database — the full script is in the README:

export ERPCRAFT_DB=/tmp/demo.db; rm -f $ERPCRAFT_DB
E="cargo run -p erpcraft-cli --"
  1. $E health and $E trial_balance — the seeded company; opening books balance at $120,500.00 per side.
  2. Configure a quote: a steel BRK-100 with a galvanized finish ($E create_quote --json '{...}', then send/accept/order). The configurator forces the reinforced mount via a named constraint and prices the line at $162.00/unit. Then ask for aluminum + galvanized via evaluate_config — it refuses, with its reason.
  3. $E agent "Invoice Acme for the 40 units we shipped Tuesday." — the agent proposes; the confirmation shows the posting rendered from the ledger (total debits $7,720.00 = total credits $7,720.00). Nothing posts until you confirm.
  4. $E agent "Can we build 200 BRK-100 by 2026-11-15?" — a live MRP plan: demand 250, 50 covered, build 200 starting Nov 10, with component purchases netted (30 steel blanks, 110 fastener sets). Push the as-of date past a start date with explain_shortage and the same plan explains the shortage instead.
  5. Release the fastener purchase through the agent — propose → confirm creates the purchase-order document; the trial balance does not move. Planning never posts.
  6. $E agent --yes "Buy $3,600 of raw materials on credit.", then $E audit_trail --source agent and $E audit_entry <id> — who posted each entry, why, the verbatim prompt, and the full provenance chain.

Also worth trying: cargo test (the invariant suite, including the oracle day), posting an unbalanced journal entry over HTTP and watching the database itself refuse it (HTTP 400), and erpcraft mcp to drive the same 85 commands as MCP tools.

And the buying side, in about a minute: $E create_supplier --name 'Harbor Components', a create_purchase_order for 10 brackets at $60.00, then $E receive_goods (watch Dr Raw Materials / Cr GR-IR post), and $E record_ap_invoice at $65.00 — the invoice lands on hold on the price variance and pay_ap_invoice refuses. Either override_ap_match with a recorded reason releases it, or just tell the agent: $E agent "Pay AP invoice 1 anyway — the price increase was approved by Dana." — the proposal shows the hold, the variance, and the override step before the payment, and posts only when you confirm.

Honest limits

  • MRP scope: no capacity planning, safety stock, or lot-sizing yet — the planner nets quantities and offsets lead times; those three are the documented next increments (see ROADMAP.md).
  • One-off prices: quote lines snapshot the item master’s price; a one-off unit price on a single line is not yet supported. The agent detects the request, flags the conflict, and refuses rather than posting a wrong price.
  • Early alpha: this is the working core of the ERP vision — ledger, order-to-cash, procure-to-pay, controls foundations, configurator, agent surface, MRP, audit — not yet the payroll, tax-engine, multi-currency, and multi-entity depth the full vision includes. Those are mapped honestly, with a build order, in ENTERPRISE_GAPS.md; the invariants they will stand on are the part that had to be right first, and they are the part that is test-proven here.
